6 Users and analytics

6.1 What we measure

aisus sends no telemetry, by design. The extension makes no network requests, and the code it maps never leaves the developer's machine. We would rather know less about our users than ask them to trust us with their code. Our numbers come from the analytics on aisus.dev, the VS Code Marketplace, and GitHub's counts of release downloads and repository visits.

6.3 Evidence

On 9 November a teardown script deleted our server and its only disk. Umami's history went with it. AWS kept the deleted server's network traffic, and section 6.4 estimates the lost visits from it.

6.4 How we estimated the lost visits

The new server lets us calibrate. On quiet days it sends about 7 MB of background traffic. Each page view adds about 0.6 MB on top. The old server sent 686 MB from 24 October to 8 November. Without the background, that is 600 to 1,000 page views. At the new site's ratio of visitors to views, it is 260 to 430 visitors.

These are bytes, not visits. The homepage changed on 2 November, and some traffic is bots and our own testing. The estimate is a range for that reason. Even its low end is four times what Umami has counted since.

6.6 What the numbers show

Prof Ben took our marketing apart after the second progress report. We took it personally. The flyers went up the day after the server launched, and on 26 October the site sent 108 MB, its biggest day on record. The first release was downloaded 29 times while the site served it.

At STePS, aisus won Best Video and Marketing. The award was judged across every project at the showcase, and CS3216 was only part of that field.

Visits turn into installs slowly. Between 330 and 500 people reached the site. GitHub served 68 release files, and the Marketplace served 115. Installing aisus takes one click. Using it needs Node.js, a signed-in Claude Code or Codex, and the user's own tokens. That setup is where we expect most people stop. The live demo in section 1.1 skips all three.
